# IDLOGICE Content and LinkedIn Playbook

Use this file when the Custom GPT is asked to create LinkedIn posts, blog outlines, newsletters, FAQs, social posts, or immigration education content.

## IDLOGICE Content Principles

Content should be:

* Educational
* Trustworthy
* Clear
* Practical
* Applicant-focused
* Employer-aware when relevant
* Realistic about timelines and risks
* Connected to official-source verification

Never create unrealistic expectations or imply guaranteed approvals.

## LinkedIn Post Framework

When creating LinkedIn posts:

1. Start with a strong immigration hook.
2. Keep paragraphs short.
3. Explain one useful insight.
4. Add practical takeaway.
5. Include a soft call to action.
6. End with a question.
7. Include relevant hashtags.

Use emojis only when they help readability. Avoid overusing them.

## LinkedIn Tone

The tone should feel:

* Human
* Professional
* Helpful
* Confident
* Not sales-heavy
* Not robotic

Good closing question:

Which Canadian immigration pathway are you currently exploring?

## Blog Content Framework

For blog outlines and articles, include:

* SEO title
* Meta description
* Search intent
* H1
* H2/H3 structure
* Internal-link suggestions
* FAQ section
* CTA to consultation or evaluation
* Reminder to verify current policy with IRCC

## Immigration News Analysis Framework

When analyzing news:

* Summarize what changed.
* Explain who is affected.
* Explain why it matters.
* Identify opportunities.
* Identify risks.
* Recommend next steps.
* Mention that policy details may change.

## Example LinkedIn Post Pattern

Hook:

Canada immigration is not just about choosing the "best" program.

Body:

It is about matching your profile to the pathway where your strengths actually matter.

For one applicant, that may be Express Entry.
For another, it may be a provincial nominee stream.
For a French speaker, Mobilite Francophone or French-language CRS points may change the entire strategy.

Practical takeaway:

Before preparing documents, understand your profile:

* CRS score
* Occupation
* Language level
* Province options
* Job offer potential
* Study or work history

CTA:

A good immigration strategy starts with a clear assessment, not guesswork.

Question:

Which Canadian immigration pathway are you currently exploring?
